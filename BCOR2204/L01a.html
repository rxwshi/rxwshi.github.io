A corporation is a legal entity separate from its owners: it can sign contracts, sue and be sued. It is set up with a charter and bylaws; shareholders elect the board, which hires managers.

A benefit corporation is a for-profit corporation that must also pursue, and report on, a public benefit.

BCOR 2204 · Lecture 1A
This is the most-tested topic in HW1 (Q3–5, Q11–17). Make sure students can:
- Identify a form from a description: one owner with full liability is a sole proprietorship; equal partners who are both fully liable is a general partnership; a separate entity with no owner liability is a corporation.
- Know that a limited partnership protects SOME owners (the limited partners) but not all.
- Know that a corporation's main advantage is raising large sums of equity, and its main disadvantage is double taxation.
- Know that an LLC gives limited liability while avoiding double taxation.
Benefit corporations are new in the updated textbook but aren't tested in HW1; mention them in one sentence. The book's state count ("as of early 2021") is dated, so the slide doesn't give one.

1 · Intro to Finance

What should the financial manager maximize?

Hands-up poll · 1.5 min
  • Maximize current profits
  • Maximize sales or market share
  • Maximize dividends
  • Minimize costs
  • Avoid bankruptcy

The problem with each: cut prices → more market share. Cut R&D → lower costs. Never borrow → no bankruptcy. Defer maintenance → higher profit this year.

Maximize the current market value per share of the existing stock
(more generally: the market value of the owners’ equity)

Current, not future. Market, not book value. Value, not profit or dividends.

BCOR 2204 · Lecture 1A
Ask for a show of hands for each option. Most students pick profit.
Reveal the problems, then the goal. Shareholders are residual claimants: if their slice grows, everyone paid ahead of them is being paid too.
HW1 Q18–20 test this exact wording, and the distractors include "future value of equity", "book value of equity", "dividends per share" and "current profits".
